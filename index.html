The process

Two minutes on how Grain works

Explainer film · 2:04 · coming soon

Prefer to read it? Here's the whole thing.

First, describe what you need. Text it, email it, or hop on a short call — whichever is least effort for you. It does not need to be organized, and it does not need to be complete. Most people send us three messy sentences and a couple of attachments, and that is genuinely enough to start from. From there we will go back and forth a bit, usually over the same day, asking the handful of questions that actually change the shape of the work. That back-and-forth is our job, not yours: you should not have to figure out what you are asking for before you are allowed to ask for it.

Then we send you an exact list of the metrics we are going to hit, along with a flat fee. Not a vague statement of work and not an hourly estimate that drifts — specific, countable things that will either be true when we are finished or will not. This is the stage where you push back. Cut a metric you do not care about, add one we missed, tighten a number until it matches what you actually need. Nothing starts until you have approved that list, and once you have, the price does not move. Later, you receive the finished work. If we miss any of the metrics on the list, you get your money back — the measurement is the whole product, so we do not get to grade our own homework.

It is a complete white-glove service. You tell us what needs to happen and we do it, including the coordination, the chasing, the forty small decisions nobody wants to own. The entire goal is to limit the amount of your time this consumes, because your time is the expensive part. There are two ways to come at it. You can give us an exact price range, and we will fit the strongest set of metrics we can inside it. Or you can give us the task with no number attached, and we will come back with the lowest price we can honestly do it for. Either way you see the full list and the full cost before anyone starts working.
